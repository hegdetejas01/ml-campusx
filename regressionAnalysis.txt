# Why ML problems are statistical inference problems?

    - "Statistical inference problem means using the samples to esitmate the paramaters of the populations"

     Consider the data of 100 students with | cgpa | iq | lpa | as the ML problem
        
        * lpa = f(cgpa, iq)

        * All students = Population
        100 examples here = Sample

        * lpa = f(cgpa, iq) + epsilon,       
            where epsilon is the error because, the lpa is also dependent other factors as well and not only on cgpa, iq (eg, on the day of the interview, the student was ill which can't be methametiacally). This error can't be reduced (IR-REDUCIBLE Error)

        Consider the funtion, f(cgpa, iq)
        Two types of functions - 
            1. Parametric
                Assumes the function is already linear
                y = w0 + w1 cgpa + w2 iq
                eg: LR

            2. Non Parametric 
                They don't make any decisions on the data 
                eg: decision tree

        * If the funtion of the population was truely linear, lpa = beta0 + beta1 cgpa + beta2 iq 
        beta0, beta1, beta2 are the paramaters of the Population (which will be represents henceforth as b0, b1, b2)

        * And I got the co_efficients through LR from the sample data I got w0, w1, w2
        where, b1, b2, b0 != w1, w2, w0
        w0, w1, w2 are different to different samples of the population

        * The equation of population is : y = b0 + b1 cgpa + b2 iq = f(cgpa, iq), f is the true relationship of x and y of the population
        * The equation of sample is : y = w0 + w1 cgpa + w2 iq = f'(cgpa, iq), f' is the relationship between x and y in the sample

        This differnce between w and b is REDUCIBLE ERROR

        * Therefore, lpa = f'(cgpa, iq) + reducible error + irreducible error

# TSS, RSS, ESS
    Data - experience and salary

    * Total Sum of Squares
        TSS = sum ((yi - ybar)**2)
        - It gives the idea of the variance of the sample data
        - It tells the overall variance in the data
        - It is the variance around the mean line

    * Residual Sum of Squares
        RSS = sum ((yi - yhat)**2)
        - Despite the regression line, we are unable to explain the error between yi and yhat, just by using the experience
        - Un Explained variance
        - It is the variance around the regression line

    * Explained Sum of Squares
        ESS = TSS - RSS
        - By applying the LR Line, we were able to explain some of the variance

    
# Degree of Freedom
    The total degree of freedom (df_total) represents the total number of data points minus 1 (n-1)
    It represents the overall variability (TSS) in the dataset that can be attributed to both the model and the residuals (RSS)

    * Total degree of freedom in LR is divided into 2 components
        df_total = df_model + df_residual

        1. df_model = number of independent variables in the model

        2. df_residual = 
            number of data points(n) - number of estimated paramaters including the intercept (k+1)
            = n - (k+1)

            It indicates the number of independent pieces of information that are available for estimating the variability in the residuals after fitting the regression model.

            We need a minimum of 3 points to have a regression line and variability in the regression line (because when there are only 2 points, the regression line passes through the points exactly and hence no variability is seen)
            Therefore, 
                when n=3, df = 1 (there is one independent point)
                    df_residual (for simple lr, k=1) = n-k-1 = 3-1-1 = 1
                when n=4, df = 2
                    df_residual (for simple lr, k=1) = n-k-1 = 4-1-1 = 2

            Consider the data in 3D, 
            to make a regression plan, we need a minimum of 3 points (which fits perfectly). Hence, we need a minimum of 4 points to have a variability
                when n=4, k=2, df_residual = n-k-1 = 4-2-1 = 3

        df_total = df_model + df_residual
                 = k + (n-k-1)
                 = n-1


# F-Test for overall significance
    * It is a test used to determine whether a LR model is statistically significant, meaning it provides a better fit to the data than just using the mean of the dependent variable

    * Eg. Has the prediction been better by adding the experience feature than when there was no experience data
